Overview

Why UAV dual cognition is necessary

Multimodal large language models have made strong progress on general image understanding, video understanding, and visual question answering, yet systematic evaluation remains limited for UAV embodied intelligence. Unlike ground-view settings, a UAV moves continuously in open 3D space and must reason not only about external targets, obstacles, and reachable directions, but also about its own position, current flight behavior, and future viewpoint changes. We therefore treat Agent-State Reasoning and World-State Reasoning as two complementary cognitive prerequisites for aerial embodied intelligence, rather than as isolated downstream tasks.

UAV-DualCog is designed around that dual-cognition view. At the capability level, it organizes the benchmark into Agent-State and World-State task lines; at the observation level, it spans both image and video settings. The benchmark is supported by a full automated toolchain that starts from semantic point clouds and landmark assets, then builds multi-view image QA, hierarchical flight-behavior video QA, and unified experiment outputs. With the current stable release, UAV-DualCog covers 12 AirSim scenes, 512 valid landmarks, 4,096 image QA samples, and 2,048 video QA samples, and exposes clear gaps between semantic answering, spatial grounding, behavior recognition, and temporal localization.

Task Definitions

The released task matrix summarizes the six benchmark tasks, their modality, cognition axis, and structured output interface.

Task Modality Cognition Output
Self-relative Position Reasoning
Infer the UAV position relative to the target landmark from a landmark-centric reference view and a current egocentric observation.
 image Agent-State Reasoning option + bbox
Action-conditioned Future Observation Prediction
Predict which candidate image is the correct future observation after a described orbit action and localize the landmark in that selected image.
 image Agent-State Reasoning option + bbox
Landmark-relative Position Reasoning
Judge where the target landmark lies relative to the UAV's current forward direction and ground it in the same observation.
 image World-State Reasoning option + bbox
Goal-conditioned Action Decision
Choose which direction the UAV should move to approach the target landmark and ground the landmark in the current observation.
 image World-State Reasoning option + bbox
Flight Behavior Recognition and Temporal Localization
Recognize the UAV's own flight behaviors from first-person video and localize the corresponding temporal intervals.
 video Agent-State Reasoning behavior option(s) + intervals
Landmark Visibility Counting and Interval Reasoning
Count landmark appearances in flight video and localize every visible interval of the target landmark.
 video World-State Reasoning count + intervals

Dual-Cognition Coordination

Paired semantic and evidence profiles across three coordination demands

Six axes report paired co-success for reciprocal position reasoning, action–observation association, and behavior–visibility consistency in matched Agent–Landmark contexts.

Coordination remains profile-dependent

The six axes organize three paired demands under the same Agent–Landmark context. Relative-position coordination tests reciprocal UAV- and landmark-centric judgments; action–observation coordination links a commanded motion to its expected view and the action required for a target view; and behavior–visibility coordination couples UAV motion with landmark appearance over time.

Semantic and evidence co-success are reported independently for each pair. A broad, balanced polygon indicates higher co-success across the measured relations, whereas a narrow spike exposes task-specific strength. Gemini 3.8 Flash is strongest on the image pairings, while the compact video axes are led by different model families.

Exact values, aligned-record counts, and the complete model table are reported in the paired audit.

Analysis

Dual-cognition analysis reveals complementary spatial and temporal capabilities

Dual cognition is the benchmark's main target, with images and videos probing complementary spatial and temporal demands. The analysis compares single-task results with Dual Semantic Acc and Dual Evidence Acc, then examines cross-media associations and the contributions of Agent-State and World-State Reasoning.

World-State semantic judgments are often stronger than Agent-State judgments, while grounding and paired success follow distinct model rankings. Human validation, diagnostic cases, open-ended questions, and reasoning-mode comparisons connect these quantitative patterns to the demands of coordinate-frame reasoning, action-observation association, and behavior-visibility consistency in UAV tasks.
