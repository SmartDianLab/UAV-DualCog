Analysis Overview

From individual task performance to grounded Agent-World coordination

We first examine image and video tasks to distinguish semantic decisions from spatial and temporal grounding, then compare performance across media. We next regroup the results by Agent-State and World-State Reasoning and examine their joint success within matched Agent-Landmark contexts. The three pairings target reciprocal coordinate-frame reasoning, action-observation association, and behavior-visibility consistency, capabilities relevant to relative-position navigation, viewpoint adjustment, and persistent target observation.

Dual Semantic Acc measures whether both paired semantic decisions are correct; Dual Evidence Acc independently measures whether both grounding outputs satisfy their task-specific thresholds. Image scores harmonically combine the two image pairings, and cross-media Overall harmonically combines image and video scores. Full task tables, correlation plots, and relation-specific profiles show how these capabilities vary across models. Human validation, failure diagnoses, open-ended questions, and same-model reasoning-mode comparisons then explain the observed patterns; dedicated pages extend the analysis to real-scene reconstructions and structured supervision.

Image Tasks

Gemini 3.8 Flash leads image Dual Semantic and Dual Evidence Acc at 44.4% and 34.3%. It also leads both Agent-State semantic tasks, while GPT-5.5 reaches the highest Self-relative Position accuracy, 65.4%. These results distinguish a strong individual answer from the ability to answer both related questions successfully.

Muse Spark reaches 64.2% and 64.4% on the World-State decisions but only 14.8% and 14.9% BBox Acc@0.5. In contrast, GLM-5.3-Flash reaches 32.7% and 33.0% World-State BBox accuracy, leading the open-weight group. Under the same evaluation protocol, specialized spatial models show no consistent advantage across all four tasks.

Conclusion and Outlook

From paired perception and reasoning to dependable UAV behavior

UAV-DualCog reveals complementary demands in Agent-State Reasoning, World-State Reasoning, and explicit spatial-temporal grounding. Model rankings differ between semantic and evidence success, while the three paired relations expose distinct strengths in coordinate-frame interpretation, action-observation association, and behavior-visibility consistency. Human, open-ended, and diagnostic analyses help explain these patterns; real-scene reconstructions extend their reach, and the optimization probe demonstrates the value of structured supervision.

Future development can build on these findings to improve models that coordinate state estimates and grounding during embodied action. Extending evaluation to physical UAV platforms and more diverse real-world scenes will connect these capabilities to relative-position navigation, subgoal completion, stopping decisions, and persistent target observation, advancing reliable aerial autonomy.
